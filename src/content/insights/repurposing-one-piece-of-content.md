Content teams often assume every post needs to start from scratch. In practice, one solid piece of source content — a client case study, a founder interview, a longer blog post — can reasonably fuel five to seven posts across a week, each in a different format, without feeling repetitive to your audience.

## Why repurposing works

Most followers don't see every single post you publish. Reformatting the same core message across a carousel, a short video, and a text post isn't redundant from the audience's perspective — for most of them, it's actually the first (and only) time they'll encounter that idea, just in whichever format they happened to scroll past.

## A simple breakdown framework

Start with the source piece and pull out its components: the single biggest insight, a supporting statistic or result, a quotable line, a step-by-step process if there is one, and a "lesson learned" angle. Each of those can become its own post — the insight as a bold-statement graphic, the statistic as a quick stat-card post, the quote as a text overlay, the process as a carousel, the lesson as a short caption-led reflection post.

A single client case study, for instance, might produce: a results-focused post ("here's what happened"), a process post ("here's how we did it"), a quote post from the client, a before/after visual if applicable, and a short video summary — five distinct posts from one piece of work.

## What to watch for

Repurposing isn't about posting the exact same graphic five times with a different caption — that does get noticed and does feel repetitive. The format has to genuinely change: different visual treatment, different length, different platform-appropriate style. The underlying idea repeats; the presentation shouldn't.

Done well, this approach solves two problems at once: it reduces the pressure to constantly generate brand-new ideas, and it makes sure your best content gets more than a single day of visibility before disappearing into the feed.
