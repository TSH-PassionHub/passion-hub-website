Being active on every platform sounds thorough, but it usually means being mediocre on all of them instead of strong on the ones that matter. Platform choice should follow audience behavior, not trend coverage — and Myanmar's platform habits look meaningfully different from what a global "best practices" guide assumes.

## Where the audience actually is

Facebook remains the default starting point for most Myanmar consumer brands — it's where discovery, customer service messages, and community building happen in one place, largely through Messenger. For brands assuming Instagram or TikTok automatically outperforms Facebook here the way it might elsewhere, that's often not the case; it depends heavily on the product category and target age group.

Viber holds a specific, practical role too — less for public content, more as a direct customer communication channel many Myanmar consumers already trust for one-to-one conversations. Brands that ignore it as "just a messaging app" are often missing a real service and sales channel their audience already expects.

## Matching platform to product

A B2B service brand and a fashion retail brand shouldn't be making the same platform decisions. Visual, aspirational categories (fashion, food, beauty) benefit more from short-form video and image-heavy platforms. Service and B2B brands often get more genuine traction from long-form Facebook posts and community group engagement than from chasing short-form trends that don't suit their sales cycle.

## The cost of spreading too thin

Every additional platform is another content format, another posting schedule, another set of analytics to actually review — not just post to. A brand posting inconsistently across five platforms typically performs worse than one posting consistently and well on two. Before adding a new platform to the mix, the useful question isn't "should we be here too?" but "do we have the capacity to be genuinely good here, not just present?"

Platform strategy should be revisited periodically, not set once and forgotten — audience habits shift, and what wasn't worth the investment two years ago might be today.
