Most delays and revision loops between a brand and their agency don't come from a lack of talent on either side — they come from a brief that left too much open to interpretation. "Make it feel premium" and "we want more engagement" are goals, not direction. An agency can't design against a feeling; it needs to know what premium looks like for your category, your competitors, and your customer.

## What a useful brief actually contains

A brief that saves everyone time answers four questions before the first draft is ever produced: who is this for, what should they do after seeing it, what has and hasn't worked before, and what's off-limits. That last one matters more than clients expect — knowing which colors, phrases, or claims are non-negotiable prevents an agency from producing beautiful work that gets rejected on a technicality.

We also ask for examples, not just descriptions. "Something like this, but not this" — pointing at a competitor's ad, a past campaign, even a completely unrelated brand whose tone you admire — gives us a reference point words alone can't. Vague adjectives are the single biggest source of wasted revision rounds.

## Where clients tend to under-brief

Budget and timeline honesty is the other common gap. Clients sometimes soften real constraints, worried that a tight budget will get them a worse creative team. In practice, the opposite happens: knowing the real number upfront lets us scope something that fits, instead of pitching an idea we later have to cut down mid-project.

The same goes for internal approval chains. If a campaign needs sign-off from three stakeholders, telling us that on day one means we plan review rounds into the timeline, rather than discovering the bottleneck the week before launch.

## The two-way part

A good brief isn't just a document you hand over — it's a conversation. We usually follow up with a short call to pressure-test the brief before work starts, because it's far cheaper to correct a misunderstanding at the whiteboard stage than after the first draft is built. If your current agency (or ours) isn't asking these questions, that's worth raising before the next project kicks off.
